Scope

One run, not a trend — and the ordering resolves less than it looks like it does. This block used to say that ordering within a category is what holds. It is the opposite way round. First place overall is separated from second place by a paired 95% interval and holds under every leave-one-out variant, though not under a single judge family scoring alone; within a category, the leader is indistinguishable from second place in most of them, because a category is questions scored on a coarse scale. Vendors the run cannot tell apart now share a rank number below, and every neighbouring pair is published with its paired interval so you can check any single claim. Individual scores carry roughly points of judge-to-judge disagreement, and the judge families differ by points on the same responses. Disagreement data.

An earlier version of this page said the ranking survives this because every vendor is scored by every judge. That is false, and the export publishes what disproves it: judge bias interacts with the vendor rather than applying equally across the row. What holds is that dropping any one judge family leaves the top of places unchanged; scored by a single family alone, of rank the vendors in a different order — on this run that reordering reaches first place. Absolute claims are softer still: "scores 8.4 out of 10" is a statement about this ensemble, not about the vendor.

The fix is a human-labelled calibration set, scored monthly against the ensemble. It is not built, and until it exists these judges are unaudited. Judge design and model pins.
