## 1. Why this plan replaces the previous four-week plan

A four-week, ~$800 validation plan was drafted that proposed building five new 120-query
distributions (legal, e-commerce, dev-docs, multilingual, real-time) across eighteen vendors,
to test whether the winning vendor changes by query distribution.

Three things were wrong with it, and this plan is the corrected version.

**1. A third of it has already been run, and it returned the kill criterion.** The export's
`build_routing_gain` measures exactly that hypothesis — each category sent to its own best
vendor, against one vendor used for everything. See §2.1. The gain is ~0 across three weeks.

**2. It proposed authoring 600 new queries.** That play was run on 2026-08-17: 240 hard-tier
candidates were authored and piloted, landed at median 8.50 against a target of 6.00, and the
tier was not promoted. Authoring harder questions is a measured failure, not an untried idea.

**3. It proposed benchmarking eighteen vendors, including four whose terms forbid it, using
rotating IPs and off-domain accounts so the vendors could not detect it.** That instruction is
struck. See §5.

What survives from that plan and is kept here: pre-registered kill criteria, a customer-free
first leg, past-behaviour interview questions, buying the vendor credits yourself, and the
insistence that a result must beat its own noise before it is called a result.

### 2.2 The nominal inversion, tested properly

W33 and W34 both show Perplexity nominally leading `code_technical` and `long_tail`. Pooling
both weeks (n = 50 paired queries per category, ~100 query-weeks), Perplexity minus Exa:

| Category | mean d | se | t | 95% CI | Separated? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **code_technical** | **+0.290** | 0.177 | 1.64 | [−0.058, +0.638] | no — closest |
| long_tail | +0.010 | 0.095 | 0.10 | [−0.177, +0.197] | no |
| breaking_news | −0.344 | 0.115 | −3.00 | [−0.568, −0.119] | **YES (Exa)** |
| general_facts | −0.100 | 0.063 | −1.59 | [−0.223, +0.023] | no |
| local_shopping | −0.050 | 0.081 | −0.62 | [−0.209, +0.109] | no |
| multi_hop | −0.060 | 0.205 | −0.29 | [−0.462, +0.342] | no |

Three readings, and they set the whole work list:

- **`long_tail` is noise.** A published "leader" ahead by 0.010 points over 100 query-weeks is
  a coin flip displayed as a ranking. That is work item **W3**.
- **`code_technical` is the one live inversion candidate.** +0.290, consistent across two
  independent weeks, CI only just crossing zero. Power calculation: sd of the paired difference
  is 0.177 × √50 = 1.252, so detecting +0.290 at 80% power and α=0.05 needs
  **n ≈ (2.802 × 1.252 / 0.290)² ≈ 146 queries**. The set currently carries 25. That is **W1**.
- **The instrument is underpowered, not broken.** `breaking_news` separates cleanly. It just
  separates in favour of the overall winner, so it confirms the method without producing an
  inversion.

| Property | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|

| Cost to run one week | $3.36 vendor + $2.07 judging ≈ **$5.45** | `vendor_spend_usd`, docs/06 |

**One trap to know about.** W31 → W33 moved *all five vendors up together* (+0.197 to +0.391,
every one significant). That was not the vendors improving — it was commit `6acb142` "Level the
payload every judge sees". A methodology change shows up as **common-mode across the panel**; a
real vendor change would show up on **one vendor**. Any comparison across those two weeks must
account for it, and any future alerting must correct for it (see W4).

### 2.4 Two failed attempts at desaturation — do not repeat them

- **Stricter rubric (2026-08-17).** Same 300 stored W31 responses, scored twice under rubrics
  differing only by an anchored 0–10 band scale. Mean 8.63 → 8.37 and share ≥9 69.0% → 56.3%,

- **Harder queries (2026-08-17).** 240 authored candidates, 40/category. Hardest 150 scored a

The untested lever both write-ups pointed at, and which this plan still does not touch:
**comparative/pairwise judging**, which `docs/04` rejected on cost rather than on
discrimination. Leave it alone until W1 and W2 report.

## 3. Hypotheses and kill criteria — pre-registered 2026-08-20

Sign and date this section before starting. Its only purpose is to stop a null result being
rationalised in week four.

> **H1 (Inversion).** For at least one real query distribution, the top-ranked vendor differs
> from the top-ranked vendor on the blended public set, by a margin that survives both a
> bootstrap confidence interval and leave-one-judge-family-out.
>
> **H2 (Willingness to pay).** Teams with meaningful search spend will pay, in advance, to have
> H1 measured on their own queries.

Both must hold. H1 without H2 is a paper. H2 without H1 is a business built on a finding that
does not survive its first technical customer.

| # | Criterion | Verdict | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | Neither probe (W1, W2) inverts under both tests | H1 dead | Publish the null. Pivot to drift, or stop |
| K2 | Inversion appears but fails leave-one-family-out | Judge artefact, not a finding | Do not sell it. Treat as K1 |
| K3 | 6 qualified conversations → 0 pre-sales | H2 dead | Stop |
| K4 | ≥3 prospects independently say "we use whatever came with the framework" | Pain not prioritised | Stop |
| K5 | Median qualified prospect's search spend < $8K/month | Pool too poor for the price | Reprice or stop |

**Go condition (all three):** inversion in ≥1 probe surviving both tests · ≥2 pre-sales from
~6 qualified conversations · ≥1 prospect volunteers drift pain before you mention it.

## 4. The work, in order

Each item states what, why, which files, and how it is validated. Per `AUTONOMY.md`: **a change
that cannot be validated by an existing gate needs a new gate first.** `scripts/check-all.sh` is
the single gate that defines "did that work".

### W1 — `code_technical` probe (highest value, do first)

**What.** Build a 150-query `code_technical` probe set and run it against **Exa and Perplexity
only**. Two vendors, because this is a two-vendor question and it costs a fifth as much.

**Why.** §2.2 — the only live inversion candidate in three weeks of data, needing n≈146 rather
than new query types.

**Do not add these queries to `src/queries/full-v1.json`.** That set is 150 queries, 25 per
category, hash `208cb6d7e33aef9b`, identical across W31/W33/W34. Its stability is what makes
the longitudinal record comparable. Unbalancing one category to 150 would break every
week-over-week comparison in §2.

**Files.** New: `src/queries/probe-code-technical.json`. Schema matches `full-v1.json`:

```json
{"set_name": "probe-code-technical",
 "note": "why this set exists, dated",
 "queries": [{"id": "ct-p001", "category": "code_technical",
              "rotates": 0, "source": "authored-probe",
              "text": "pgbouncer transaction pooling prepared statements error"}]}
```

Write real developer queries — exact-match retrieval, version-sensitive, where official docs and
GitHub issues should beat SEO content. Not benchmark-style questions.

**Cost.** 150 × 2 = 300 responses. At the measured $0.0045/response vendor + $0.0028/response
judging ≈ **$2.20**.

**Validation, both required:**
1. Bootstrap the 150 queries (1,000 resamples) → CI on the paired Perplexity − Exa difference.
2. Recompute under each leave-one-judge-family-out panel (`build_robustness` already does this
   at top level; apply the same three panels here). **The inversion must hold in all three.**

**Report.** Mean d, se, t, 95% CI, and the three per-panel results. If the CI excludes zero in
all three panels, H1 has survived its first test.

### W2 — multilingual probe

**What.** 120 queries — roughly 24 each in Spanish, German, Japanese, Russian and Portuguese —
spanning the same query classes as the main set. Run against all five cleared vendors.

**Why.** It is the one axis nothing in the 150-query set touches, and the one where inversion is
most plausible and would be largest: index coverage by language varies enormously, and a vendor
with thin Japanese coverage is not "slightly worse", it is unusable at any price. That also
defeats the otherwise-fatal objection that when vendors are near-equal on quality and differ 7–23×
on price, the rational answer is "buy the cheapest adequate one" — which is already free.

**Files.** New: `src/queries/probe-multilingual.json`. Add a `lang` field per query.

**Cost.** 120 × 5 = 600 responses ≈ **$4.40**.

**Validation.** Same two tests as W1, plus report per-language as well as pooled — a single
language inverting is a more useful and more sellable finding than a pooled average.

**Note on the judges.** All three judge models are multilingual, but judge–human calibration
(79.1%) was measured on English only. **Say so in any write-up.** If W2 produces the finding the
whole plan rests on, a small non-English calibration pass becomes the next priority, not an
optional extra.

### W3 — stop the per-category leader over-claiming

**What.** `build_routing_gain` emits `leaders` — one named vendor per category — and the site
displays it. In `long_tail` that leader is ahead by 0.010 points. Emit a separation verdict
alongside each leader and have the site render a tie where the leader is not separated from the
runner-up.

**Why.** It is the same failure the heatmap commit (`02994ae`) already fixed once, and it is the
first thing a technically literate prospect will check. `docs/13` rule 8 and the "no figure is
typed by hand" rule in `CLAUDE.md` are the same principle applied to numbers; this applies it to
rankings.

**Files.** `src/export.py` (`build_routing_gain` around line 491, alongside `build_tiers` line
397 which already computes separation); `site/assets/site.js` `derived()`; the category section
of `site/results.html` and `site/index.html`.

**Gate — write this first.** Add a check to `scripts/check-quality.mjs` that fails the build if
any category names a leader without an accompanying separation verdict. Without the gate this
regresses silently, which is how the cadence claim and the calibration claim both went stale.

**Validate.** `bash scripts/check-all.sh` must pass, including the new check, and the new check
must be shown to fail against the current export before the fix lands.

### W4 — common-mode correction before any alerting

**What.** When comparing a vendor across weeks, report its change **relative to the panel mean**,
not in absolute points.

**Why.** §2.3 — W31→W33 moved all five vendors +0.3 on a methodology change. Uncorrected, the
first alert any monitoring product ever sends will be about its own deploy. A vendor-side change
appears on one vendor; a measurement-side change appears on all of them.

**Files.** `src/export.py`, alongside `build_robustness` (line 689).

**Do not build a monitoring product on the back of this.** W4 is a correctness fix to the
existing export. The evidence for monitoring as a *product* is weak — three weeks show zero
vendor movement relative to the panel — and it is being validated in Leg 2 as a question, not

## 5. Constraints — these are not negotiable by an agent

1. **Never hide benchmark traffic from a vendor.** No rotating IPs, no off-domain mystery-shopper
   accounts, no evasion of detection. That converts a terms disagreement you could defend in
   public into bad faith you cannot. The entire asset is being the party nobody can dismiss.
2. **Do not add Tavily, Brave, Seltz or Search Router** to any published run until written consent

3. **Do not modify `src/queries/full-v1.json` or `src/queries/heldout/manifest.json`.** Probes are
   separate sets. The weekly set's stability is the longitudinal record.
4. **Do not write a cadence claim by hand, in either direction.** It derives from `runs.trigger`.
5. **Do not type a figure into HTML.** Every number resolves from the export via `data-val`;
   `scripts/check-site.mjs` fails the build otherwise.
6. **Do not dispatch the weekly workflow.** It spends real money every Monday and an unattended
   dispatch can collide with the scheduled run.
7. **Publish the null if it is a null.** A benchmark publishing a result that undercuts its own
   commercial thesis is the single most credible thing this project can do, and it costs nothing
   that was not already lost.

## 8. Reproducing the analysis in this document

Save as `experiments/pooled_category_test.py` and run from the repository root. It regenerates
§2.2, and the same loader regenerates the paired week-over-week figures in §2.3.

```python
import csv, math, statistics as st
from collections import defaultdict

def load(week):
    per, cat = defaultdict(list), {}
    with open('site/export/judge-scores-' + week + '.csv', newline='') as f:
        for r in csv.DictReader(f):
            if r['held_out'] == '1':
                continue
            per[(r['query_id'], r['vendor'])].append(float(r['overall']))
            cat[r['query_id']] = r['category']
    return {k: st.median(v) for k, v in per.items()}, cat

a, cat = load('2026-W33')
b, cat2 = load('2026-W34')
cat.update(cat2)

# Perplexity minus Exa, paired by query, both weeks pooled.
# Pooling is legitimate here only because query_set_hash is identical across
# W31/W33/W34 (208cb6d7e33aef9b) — check that before pooling any other pair.
for c in ['code_technical', 'long_tail', 'general_facts',
          'breaking_news', 'local_shopping', 'multi_hop']:
    d = [W[(q, 'perplexity')] - W[(q, 'exa')]
         for W in (a, b)
         for (q, v) in W
         if v == 'exa' and cat.get(q) == c and (q, 'perplexity') in W]
    m = st.mean(d)
    sd = st.pstdev(d) * math.sqrt(len(d) / (len(d) - 1))
    se = sd / math.sqrt(len(d))
    lo, hi = m - 1.96 * se, m + 1.96 * se
    print('%-16s n=%d  d=%+.3f  se=%.3f  CI[%+.3f,%+.3f]  %s'
          % (c, len(d), m, se, lo, hi, 'SEP' if lo > 0 or hi < 0 else 'ns'))
```

## 9. One-page summary

> **Test 1 (week 1, ~$17, no customers).** Two probes, not five distributions: 150
> `code_technical` queries against Exa and Perplexity — the only live inversion candidate in
> three weeks of data, needing n≈146 to separate — and 120 multilingual queries against all five
> cleared vendors, the one axis never tested. An inversion counts only if the bootstrap CI
> excludes zero **and** it survives all three leave-one-judge-family-out panels.
>
> **Test 2 (weeks 2–7).** Publish the result either way. Then build the list of the ~500
> companies worldwide spending ≥$8K/month on search, send 300–400 personal emails, and ask every
> qualified conversation for $5,000.
>
> **Go:** an inversion that survives both tests, plus 2 people who have paid.
> **Stop:** either fails. Cost of finding out: seventeen dollars and seven weeks.
