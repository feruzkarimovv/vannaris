# Vannaris — full product and direction audit, 2026-08-13

Commissioned by the founder with the instruction "be brutally honest; the aim is a startup and
fundraising, not a side project." Method: direct review of the full research record (docs/01–15),
both handoffs, the applications, the live data, and the CI history, plus six parallel deep
investigations — harness code and CI, legal posture, W31-vs-W33 data analysis, live-site and
redesign review, external market re-sweep (the last one was 2026-07-30), and the remaining project
record. Everything below is sourced to a file, a computed number, or a dated external reference.
Nothing in the working tree was modified.

---

## 1. Executive verdict

**Keep the benchmark. Stop building the commercial layer on speculation. Treat distribution as the
job. And move within weeks, not months — the whitespace this project was scoped against began
closing today.**

The instrument is the most epistemically honest artifact I have seen at this stage of a company:
self-correcting, gate-enforced, publishing the findings that undercut its own thesis. That is a
real, fundable asset — as a founder-quality signal and as the seed of a trust franchise. But it is
attached to: zero users, zero outreach ever sent (to vendors *or* to the identified customer pool),
zero revenue, a repository with 0 stars, a monetization story that has retreated three times
without touching a customer, a core differentiator ("continuously run") that has failed to start
three weeks in a row with a permanent hole in the history, and a measuring instrument that is
saturating against its own 10-point ceiling. Meanwhile, on the day of this audit, a competitor
(NativePort) posted an 11-vendor web-search-API leaderboard with an attached routing gateway to
Hacker News.

The failure mode is not being wrong. It is being perfectly right in private while the category
gets named by someone louder.

## 4. The instrument: what the data actually says now

Computed fresh from `site/data/2026-W31.json`, `2026-W33.json`, and the export CSVs.

**Saturation is real and worsening.** Share of (query, vendor) ensemble medians ≥9: 61.4% (W31) →
70.1% (W33); exactly 10: 22.5% → 26.0%. Four of five vendors have a *median* score of 9; Exa lands
≥9 on 92% of its scored W33 cells. The instrument is pressed against its ceiling, which mechanically
compresses the differences it exists to measure.

**Nothing moves, and what movement exists is judge drift, not vendor change.** W31→W33 (same
query-set hash, methodology v2 in between): every vendor rose in lockstep, +0.20 to +0.39; zero
rank changes. Consistent with v2 judge leniency, not with any vendor getting better. A weekly
benchmark whose table is identical every week has no reason to be visited weekly.

**Separation is close to gone at the top.** Category leaders separable from second place: 2 of 6
(W31) → **1 of 6** (W33). The Exa–Perplexity gap collapsed from 0.278 (significant) to 0.075 (not);
they now share tier 1. ~68% of queries have a tied winner in both weeks.

**Routing on quality is confirmed dead on this instrument; routing on cost is modest but real.**
Quality gain of a per-category oracle over all-Exa: 0.000 (W31), 0.017 (W33). A cost-aware policy
(cheapest vendor within 0.5 points of the category leader, PAYG rates) yields $0.0057/query vs.
all-Exa $0.0070 — **18.6% cheaper at −0.12 quality**. All-Serper is 86% cheaper at −0.78. That is
the entire quantified value proposition of any routing product on current data: a modest cost
optimization, not a quality play.

**W33's data quality regressed, with a bias-shaped hole.** Completeness fell 95.6% → 89.3%; the
OpenAI judge failed 77 calls (vs. 33), and — the important part — **its failures concentrate on
long responses from Exa (26), Linkup (25), and Perplexity (21), versus Serper (3) and You.com
(5)**. Since only complete ensembles publish, Exa's W33 score rests on 124/150 queries with its
longest answers selectively dropped. That is a length-correlated selection bias affecting exactly
the vendors being compared at the top, and a sophisticated critic would find it in the public CSV.

**The held-out set has never actually run.** Registered 2026-08-04, hash committed, gated in CI,
described on the site — and `n_heldout_queries` is 0 in both published weeks, with `heldout: null`.
Its 4-week rotation deadline (2026-09-01) is approaching for a set that has produced zero data.
Why today's manual W33 run installed 0 held-out questions needs investigating (secret missing in
Actions? install step silently no-op?) before the next run.

**Calibration is exactly where it was on 2026-08-05.** The pairwise task is designed (280 pairs,
set `96afde9bfef3`, in the DB since 2026-08-05), the UI is built, the gate exists — and zero pair
labels have been collected; the task directory doesn't even exist on disk and must be regenerated.
Total human labels ever: 15. The site's honest "judges are unaudited" caveat is doing permanent
duty for what was planned as a temporary state.

**Verdict on the instrument:** the plumbing is trustworthy; the measurement is running out of
headroom. A harder query set is not polish — it is the difference between a benchmark and a
participation trophy, and it is also the only thing that could revive cadence-interest (movement),
separation (meaning), and possibly even the routing thesis (differences worth routing on).

## 5. Operations: the differentiator has not started

The entire claim against prior art is "continuously updated, scheduler-invoked, history published."
The record: **Aug 3** scheduled run died on an Anthropic credit preflight (spent nothing). **Aug
10** ran 15 minutes, spent the vendor money, then failed on 336 OpenAI 429s. **W32 is a permanent
hole** — the one asset with no recovery path. **Aug 13** (today): a manual dispatch succeeded and
published W33. `scheduled_weeks` = 0, `schedule_started` = false, six weeks after the runner was
built.

Today's two fixes (real-call judge preflight; per-family rate pacing) plausibly close the Aug 10
failure class. The code audit found what they do *not* close:

- **Nothing persists until the whole judging stage completes** (`runner.py:551`). A crash, stall,
  or the 90-minute job timeout mid-judging discards the entire run *and* the `--rejudge` recovery
  path with it, because there is no stored run to re-judge. This is the largest structural gap.
- **Quota-exhaustion 429s are indistinguishable from rate-limit 429s** (`ensemble.py:394`) — an
  `insufficient_quota` response gets 70-second retry sleeps while holding a concurrency slot; at
  scale this can stall past the timeout, hitting the bullet above.
- **The preflight proves a key works, not that it can afford the run** — one 8-token probe passes
  on a balance that dies at call 400 (the Aug 3/Aug 10 shape, generalized). It also doesn't send
  the real call's parameters (`temperature`, `seed`, `response_format`), so a model that rejects
  any of them passes preflight and fails 100% of judging.
- **The weekly workflow never runs `scripts/check-all.sh`** — the publishing path skips the unit
  tests and several gates that only run on push/PR.
- Pacing was measured for 750 judge calls; a run with the held-out set is 900. `PRICING`
  (`export.py:99`), on which the headline cost claim rests, is hand-maintained with no freshness
  check. Nothing alarms if a provider silently swaps the pinned judge model
  (`judge_model_returned` is stored but never checked). GitHub disables cron after 60 days without
  repo activity, and bot commits don't reliably reset that clock.

Monday 2026-08-17 should be treated as a launch: watched live, with the founder able to top up any
of the eight accounts mid-run.

## 6. Legal: excellent research, unsent envelopes

The research (docs/03, 11, 14, 15) is thorough and honest. The gap between analysis and action is
where all the actual risk lives. Ranked by likelihood × impact:

1. **Exa §4.2 — the live one.** §4.2(a) bars publishing "any information … obtained from or
   through the Services" without **written permission**; §4.2(f) bars using Output "to develop any
   competitive product" — arguably reaching the planned router. Exa is currently ranked #1, which
   helps commercially, but the exposure is a named clause, a live site publishing against it, and
   a **drafted permission email that has been sitting unsent since 2026-08-07**
   (`outreach/vendor-permission-2026-08-07.md`, header: "not sent"). Cheapest fix in this entire
   audit: send it.
2. **Perplexity's terms have never been read by anyone on this project.** Every automated fetch
   403s; the current row rests on a secondary reading from 2026-07-30; the terms were updated
   2026-01-23 and a separate Search-API addendum exists, unread. A vendor is on a public
   leaderboard under a contract nobody has seen. This is a five-minute manual browser task.
3. **`LICENSE-DATA` sublicenses vendor-derived figures to the world under CC BY 4.0, commercial
   use permitted.** Onward *licensing* is a stronger act than the "publish derived scores only"
   boundary was designed to justify, and it sits directly against Exa §4.2(a) and Linkup's
   internal-needs-only Answers licence. docs/15 asks about publishing but never about licensing
   onward. Needs a decision (and probably the attorney).

Also material: the **router, not the benchmark, carries the sharpest contractual exposure** —
Linkup Art. 6.4 names "acting as a wrapper of the Linkup APIs" verbatim as a prohibited competing
service, and Exa §4.2(f) reaches Output-derived products. Any future router build has to be
designed around those two clauses, not just around BYOK. The You.com T&C-vs-MSA question (which
governs depends on how the account was opened — only the founder knows) determines whether the
favourable Output-assignment applies and whether publishing You.com's per-query price breaches MSA
§7.1. Tavily/Brave consent emails: drafted, verified addresses, never sent. Attorney read:
deferred 2026-08-07, "deferred, not declined" — every week of growing visibility raises what that
deferral is worth. Name: "Vannaris" is clean on every register checked (USPTO exact-match: 0 live,
0 dead), with the phonetic/similar-mark search and the `.ai` domain still open.

## 7. The site — and a redesign that quietly changes the company

The **live** site is correct and honest: cadence copy derives from `track_record` (it says
scheduled runs haven't started), the routing-gain-≈0 admission is on the index, the limitations
section is prominent, no hand-typed figures, gates pass.

The **uncommitted redesign** in the working tree (index.html −739/+110) converts the homepage from
"independent benchmark" into a **private-evals sales page**: new title "Vannaris: private evals for
the search APIs your agents call," CTA "Get in touch," and it drops from the landing page the
limitations section, the headline finding, the routing-honesty callout, the COI link, the
Tavily/Brave exclusion note, "No vendor pays to be here," and the published-weeks metric. It also
sells a "Live score feed — fresh scores each cycle" while `schedule_started` is false — skirting
via product copy the exact cadence rule the project enforces in code.

Stated plainly: **the redesign makes the site look like the genre of page this project exists to
be the alternative to.** The trust surface is the moat; the sales pitch has to sit on top of it,
not replace it. A visitor should hit the finding and the table in ten seconds (the redesign's
brevity is right), with the disclosures visible and the private-evals CTA secondary. Recommend not
shipping it as-is. (Also missing on both versions: robots.txt and sitemap.xml — trivial, worth
adding before any launch.)

## 8. The business model: three retreats, zero contact

The record, honestly stated: quality routing (killed by the project's own data — gain 0.000/0.017);
reliability-layer client (docs/02 itself concedes the client layer is commoditized by free
LiteLLM); live score feed (only worth paying for if the scores are trusted *and keep changing* —
§4 says they don't); private evals (the best remaining hypothesis — and zero have been sold,
pitched, or even discussed with a prospective buyer).

The structural problem: **the benchmark's current finding is a sentence, not a subscription.**
"Serper is 92–98% of Exa's quality on four of six categories at 1/7th the price" is read once and
acted on once. Recurring revenue requires either the answer changing over time (needs the harder
query set) or the evaluation being customer-specific (private evals — needs distribution and
trust). No amount of further building resolves which of these someone will pay for. Only customers
can, and none has been asked. The LiteLLM issue #15314 commenters — identified as the
pre-qualified pain pool in July's research — have never been contacted.

The cost side is a genuine strength: the whole operation runs at ~$24/month ($3.37 vendor +
~$2 judges per run, static hosting), so the track record accrues on a personal card regardless of
any funding outcome.

## 12. Kill criteria and change-my-mind criteria

**Evidence that should end the project** (set in advance, per this project's own habits): a real
launch and 4+ weeks of genuine outreach producing no traffic spike, no signups/stars/replies from
the pain pool, and zero private-eval interest — that would falsify "the market wants this
measurement from a neutral party," which is the load-bearing thesis. Walk away with an excellent
portfolio piece and a true story.

**Evidence that should upgrade the ambition:** any vendor publicly citing or disputing a score
(both are wins); a hard-query-set run in which categories have *different*, separable leaders
(revives routing); a paid eval that renews; NativePort or a peer gaining traction with a
non-neutral leaderboard (proves the category will cite *a* number — the neutral one then has a
target to beat).

**The one-sentence summary:** the instrument deserves to exist, the company hasn't been attempted
yet, and the next eight weeks should be spent making the second true rather than making the first
more perfect.
